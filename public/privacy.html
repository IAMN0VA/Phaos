Medical imaging can contain highly sensitive personal information. Treat every study as private, even if visible identifiers appear to have been removed.

What this notice covers

This notice describes the privacy design of the standard browser-based viewer. A school, imaging company, distributor, hospital, or other organization that deploys a modified or separately hosted version may have additional privacy obligations and should provide its own notice where required.

Medical-image processing

The standard viewer is designed to read selected DICOM and image files inside the user's browser session. The application does not require the operator to receive or permanently store the selected medical-image files in order to render them. Decoded pixel data, DICOM metadata needed for rendering, measurements, markers, and viewer state may exist temporarily in browser or GPU memory while the session is open.

Network information

Loading a website normally requires network requests to the hosting provider. The host may therefore receive ordinary technical information such as IP address, browser or device information, request time, and requested application files through standard server or security logs. The standard PHAOS build does not include advertising, third-party analytics, behavioral tracking, crash-reporting SDKs, or remote-font requests.

Session storage

The viewer may use browser session storage for non-medical interface preferences such as the selected experience (Explore, Study, or Demo). The standard build does not intentionally store the selected DICOM study in persistent browser storage. Choosing New Study, refreshing the page, or closing the tab clears the active in-memory viewer session, subject to normal browser and operating-system behavior.

Exports and screenshots

Structured JSON export uses a minimized schema that intentionally omits common direct identifiers, filenames, accession numbers, Study/Series/SOP UIDs, and raw DICOM metadata. This does not guarantee that an export is anonymous. User-entered marker labels can contain identifying text, and identifiers can be burned directly into image pixels. Screenshot export therefore warns users to review the image before sharing it.

Your responsibility when opening a study

Only open imaging that you are authorized to access. Avoid identifiable studies on shared or public computers. For classroom, conference, product-demonstration, or partner-client use, de-identified teaching data should be used whenever practical and required by the applicable organization or law.

Support and voluntary communications

If you voluntarily contact the operator, the information you send may be processed in order to respond. Do not send identifiable medical images, reports, or patient information through ordinary support channels unless an authorized secure process has been provided. The in-app diagnostic copy function is designed to exclude patient names, filenames, DICOM UIDs, accession numbers, and raw DICOM metadata.

Partner deployments

An imaging company or other partner may provide this viewer to customers as a supplemental educational or technical-visualization resource. If a partner adds accounts, cloud storage, telemetry, remote study retrieval, analytics, or other network services, the partner and operator must update the privacy disclosures and contractual controls before those features are used.

Retention

The standard viewer does not intentionally retain uploaded medical-image files on an application server because the files are not required to be uploaded there. Hosting, security, and support logs may have separate retention periods controlled by the production operator or hosting provider and should be documented before public launch.

Contact and privacy rights

The production site should identify the legal operator and provide a privacy contact before public launch. Users may contact that operator regarding privacy questions or applicable access, correction, or deletion rights. Applicable obligations depend on jurisdiction, deployment model, and whether an organization is acting in a healthcare, educational, consumer, or commercial context.
